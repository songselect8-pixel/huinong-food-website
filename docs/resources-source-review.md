## Sources actually read

1. Utah State University Extension, Buying a Home Freeze-Dryer. Read the process and safety sections. Used only for sublimation/dry texture and the stated limitation that freeze drying is not a bacterial kill step. Home equipment figures, storage-life examples, prices and process times were not copied into the guides.
2. FAO/WHO Codex Alimentarius CXS 69-1981, Quick-Frozen Raspberries, via the official FAO PDF proxy. Read scope, presentation and quality definitions. Its stated scope excludes products indicated for industrial processing. Used as vocabulary and scope context, not as a universal commercial whole/broken/crumble classification. No percentages, AQLs, temperatures or defect limits adopted as FRUNORIA commitments.
3. USDA AMS U.S. Standards for Grades of Frozen Blueberries, effective 1957, corrected/reprinted 2026-04-24. Read voluntary status, types, quality factors and defects. Only identity/quality vocabulary used; no grade claim or fixed numerical specification.
4. Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, Raspberry. Read botanical name and leaf morphology. Used for identity discussion and illustrative leaf form. Health/medicinal folklore was excluded. Actual offered species remains unverified.
5. European Commission Food Contact Materials overview. General EU framework checked 2026-10-04; article explicitly dates this check. No material-specific compliance decision, complete labelling advice or blanket packaging capability claim.
6. ISO Certification page. Read certification/verification explanation, including that ISO itself does not certify organizations. No certificate ownership, accreditation claim, issuer logo or product approval inferred.

Each source link appears near the supported statement and in the corresponding article references. The remainder of the text is original procurement questions and project checklists, not copied supplier specifications.
